\begin{definition}[Viscosity super-solution, Definition~2.1]
Let $\mathbb G$ be a Carnot setting (\noderef{CarnotSetting}) with horizontal frame
$\sigma$, let $A:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^{m\times m}$ and $H:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R$, let
$\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ and let $v:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$. We say that $v$ is a
\emph{viscosity super-solution of $\mathcal Lv=0$ in $\Omega$}, written
$\mathrm{IsViscSupersol}\ \mathbb G\ A\ H\ \Omega\ v$, if
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v$ is lower semi-continuous on $\Omega$; and
\item for every $x\in\Omega$ and every $\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)$ with
  $x\in\mathrm{argmin}_\Omega(v-\varphi)$, every second-order jet $(D,B)$ of $\varphi$ at
  $x$ (\noderef{TwiceDiffAt}) with $\mathrm{hgrad}\ \sigma\ x\ D\neq0$ satisfies
  \[
   \mathrm{Lop}\ \sigma\ A\ H\ x\ D\ B\ \ge\ 0 ,
  \]
  with $\mathrm{hgrad}$ as in \noderef{hgrad} and $\mathrm{Lop}$ as in \noderef{Lop}.
\end{enumerate}
Here $x\in\mathrm{argmin}_\Omega(v-\varphi)$ means, as in (2.8), that $x\in\Omega$
and $(v-\varphi)(x)\le(v-\varphi)(y)$ for every $y\in\Omega$.

This is the super-solution half of Definition Definition~2.1 for the operator (1.8): the
admissible test functions are those of the class $\mathcal A^{-}_x(v,\Omega)$, defined as
in (2.11) with $\mathrm{argmin}$ in place of $\mathrm{argmax}$, and the required
inequality is $\mathcal L\varphi(x)\ge0$. As for sub-solutions, $\mathcal L\varphi(x)$ is
written through the second-order jet of $\varphi$ at $x$, which for $\Omega$ open,
$\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)$ and $x\in\Omega$ exists, is unique, and equals
$(\nabla\varphi(x),D^2\varphi(x))$;
the condition $\mathcal X\varphi(x)\neq0$ keeps $A$ and $H$ away from the origin; and the
operator carries no zeroth-order term. Writing $\mathcal Lv\ge0$ in $\Omega$ in the
viscosity sense means that $v$ is a viscosity super-solution in this sense. The notion is only ever used with $\Omega$ an open set, as in
Definition Definition~2.1, which is what makes the quantification over second-order jets of
$\varphi$ at $x$ non-vacuous. A viscosity solution is a function that is both a
sub-solution and a super-solution.
\end{definition}
